Decision exercise · About 8 minutes

Would you expand the quote follow-up pilot?

You’re the commercial director. In three pilot offices, a sales rep calls the referring agent two business days after sending a new-business quote if it is still open. Elsewhere, reps choose when to follow up. Sales wants every office to adopt the call. Use the agreed OKR below to decide where, and in what form, the practice should continue.

Objective
Grow new insurance business that customers choose to renew.
Key Result
Increase qualified new annual policies issued by the three pilot offices from 1,000 to 1,150 in Q1 2027.
Guardrail
Review the rollout if first renewal falls more than two percentage points below the comparable prior-year Q1 cohort.

Facilitator view. Before each reveal, ask the group what reps should do about the scheduled calls and which evidence supports that choice. You can enter a group decision or leave the fields blank. The next report is available when you choose to reveal it.

Reading the two shares. Both shares include early cancellations in the denominator. The premium share divides the issue premium on policies that renewed by the issue premium on all qualifying policies. Repricing at renewal cannot change that calculation.

Guardrail finding. The policy renewal rate fell four percentage points, from 86% to 82%, crossing the 84% lower boundary. The share of original premium on renewing policies rose five points. The count-based Guardrail requires the director to review the call rule.

What the premium adds

The team wrote 15% more policies at an average premium 12% lower. Together those changes lifted written premium to $2.53 million. Keeping up with inflation would have taken $2.55 million. A smaller policy can be worth pursuing if the coverage fits the customer and commission income covers the service effort. The reports give you the premium. You would need commission and cost data to judge contribution.

The 207 campaign policies that did not renew accounted for $253,000 of original premium, about $1,222 per policy. The 943 that renewed averaged about $2,415 at issue. Ask what happened to the smaller policies, including whether product or channel mix explains the gap. The director still owes a decision on the policy-count Guardrail.

Did the OKR guide the decision?

The Key Result recorded the issuance gain. The Guardrail brought the director back when renewal fell. Both measures did their job. In Chapter 7, the director pauses expansion and asks sales and underwriting to check coverage fit, pricing, and product mix. In this exercise, you can also compare similar quotes handled with scheduled calls and with rep-timed follow-up. The office totals alone cannot tell you which practice helped customers.

Discuss together. If you retired the call rule tomorrow, what other approach would you test to help customers choose coverage they will renew? A separate reason to revisit the OKR itself would be evidence that its measures systematically miss an outcome the team cares about.
