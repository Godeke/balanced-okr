# Chapter 7 — Operating Balanced OKRs

The framework should improve decisions, not create a second management bureaucracy. A useful operating rhythm is light enough to sustain and rigorous enough to detect bad tradeoffs.

## Drafting

Start with the business outcome and the person accountable for it. Establish a baseline before choosing an ambitious target. Define the measurement population, data source, time window, and owner. Then identify one or two material adverse effects, and decide whether they require hard boundaries, review triggers, or contextual monitoring.

Choose diagnostics only if someone will use them to investigate variation or make a decision. If the dashboard has fifty numbers and no decision attached to them, it is not balanced; it is cluttered.

## Approval

Review the objective at both team and portfolio levels. Check whether the outcome matters, whether the KR is a credible measure, whether guardrails protect against major harms, and whether cross-team commitments fit capacity. Ask what the team would do if the KR improves but the guardrail deteriorates.

A review should be able to reject an objective that is easy to score but unimportant.

## In-quarter review

Use a short cadence suited to the work. Discuss evidence, not ceremonial red/yellow/green status. A useful sequence is: what changed, what did we expect, what surprised us, what risks emerged, and what decision follows?

Where a guardrail triggers, verify data quality and investigate cause. A significant breach should require a decision, not a silent exception. Log the decision and accountable approver.

## Scoring without false precision

A single percentage can conceal very different stories. Report at least: **KR progress**, **guardrail status**, **evidence confidence**, and **learning/next decision**. These are distinct dimensions, not inputs to an arbitrary weighted average.

For example, “KR achieved; guardrail breached; outcome under review” is more informative than 0.83. Likewise, “KR missed; hypothesis rejected with strong evidence” can be a valuable discovery result.

## Retrospectives and retirement

At quarter end, examine outcome quality, assumptions, behavior changes, cross-team effects, and delayed follow-up. Retire metrics that no longer discriminate between meaningful success and superficial improvement.

Feedback to employees should be continuous. OKR reviews should not replace coaching, fair promotion criteria, performance documentation, or necessary corrective action.

## A minimal record

A one-page OKR record should include: objective, owner, period, rationale, key results with definitions, guardrails with triggers, diagnostics, dependencies, assumptions, and next review date. Add a decision log only when something consequential changes.

**Practice exercise:** Replace a numerical OKR grade with a four-line narrative: result, side effects, confidence, and next decision. Compare how much more useful the discussion becomes.
