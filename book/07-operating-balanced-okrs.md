# Chapter 7 — Operating Balanced OKRs

A team should be able to hand its quarter-end record to the person who will see next year's renewals. That person needs to know what the team achieved, which question was still open, and who agreed to act on the answer. Use the [blank Balanced OKR worksheet](worksheet.md) for both reviews.

## Before approving the work

Start with the Objective and one accountable owner. For each Key Result, agree on the population, baseline, target, deadline, and source. Ask a frontline colleague how someone could hit the number while the customer or another team absorbed the cost. Use that answer to choose a Guardrail with a defined boundary or review trigger. A breakdown by channel or service belongs under the Guardrail when it helps assess that risk.

Agree on who can act and what work can proceed while evidence is pending. The person who can authorize expansion should hear directly from any team whose capacity it will require. An engineering participant can commit a delivery date or identify which other request would move. If the tradeoff is too costly, revise the proposal before approval.

## A completed review

In the hypothetical insurance case, the commercial director owns the expansion decision. The Objective is to grow new insurance business that customers choose to renew. Sales aims to issue 1,150 qualified new annual policies in Q1 2027, up from 1,000 in the prior quarter. Before launch, the team agrees to review expansion if first renewal falls more than two percentage points below the comparable prior-year Q1 cohort. Each cohort includes all originally issued qualified new annual policies, including those that cancel early.

| Review date | KR actual vs. target | Guardrail finding and evidence | Decision, reason, and follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| March 31, 2027 | 1,150 issued against a target of 1,150. KR achieved. | **Pending.** These annual policies have yet to reach first renewal. The overall judgment is provisional. | The commercial director keeps the campaign at its current scope and owns the renewal review by April 30, 2028. Only the director may authorize early expansion. Any such decision records the scope, reason, and revisit date. |
| April 30, 2028 | The quarter's issuance result still stands. | **Breached.** First renewal was 82% against 86% for the comparison cohort, a four-percentage-point decline. | The director pauses expansion. Underwriting will check product mix and renewal pricing. Sales will sample files for evidence of poor fit. The director will review those findings on May 31 before deciding whether to resume or change the campaign. |

The director can pause as soon as the figures are confirmed. Understanding the cause takes longer. Premium changes across a product line would call for a different response than evidence that buyers were sold unsuitable coverage. A suspected cause stays a question until someone checks it.

## Between reviews

Meet when there is evidence or a decision that warrants attention. Compare what happened with what the team expected. Check the denominator and the observation window if a number looks surprising. When a material business threshold is crossed, the decision owner records the response and the reason. Continuing the work does not erase a breach. Legal, safety, and compliance obligations remain binding.

Keep feedback to individuals timely. A manager can recognize the judgment of the salesperson who raised the renewal concern while the director investigates the campaign. Using quarterly scores to rank employees would give people a reason to stay quiet.

## At the next planning review

Revisit a recurring measure with the people whose work it affects. Can they now improve it without producing the outcome? Has a change in customers or systems weakened the relationship? Keep, revise, or retire it, and record a reason the next owner can use. The same question applies to the worksheet itself. If a field has no bearing on this decision, leave it out.

**Practice exercise.** Fill out the worksheet for a decision coming up this quarter. Give it to the colleague who would cover the next review in your absence. Can they tell when they may act, and when they must bring the decision back?
