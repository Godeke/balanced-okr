# Chapter 4 — Causality, Evidence, and Discovery

An activity is not an outcome. A correlation is not necessarily a causal effect. Balanced OKRs should preserve these distinctions because organizations frequently turn plausible stories into operational requirements before testing them.

## A chain of assumptions

Consider agent phone calls. The proposed chain may be: calls produce conversations; conversations reveal needs; better understanding produces suitable quotes; suitable quotes improve conversion and retention. Each link can fail, and the strength of each link may vary by account size or product complexity.

Measuring the first link alone tells us little about the last. An intervention that works for complex commercial accounts may waste effort on small, straightforward policies.

## Execution and discovery OKRs

An **execution OKR** is appropriate when there is credible evidence that an intervention works and the challenge is implementation. A **discovery OKR** is appropriate when the main uncertainty is whether, where, or why an intervention works.

For discovery, a valid outcome can be a decision-quality result: estimate an effect, narrow uncertainty, or identify a segment in which an intervention is not worthwhile. Define in advance what evidence would change the decision. Do not turn a null result into a post-hoc success claim without acknowledging limitations.

## Comparison groups and confounding

If customers who receive calls renew more often, perhaps calls help. Or perhaps agents call their most valuable accounts, which were more likely to renew anyway. A simple comparison confounds the treatment with account selection.

Where ethical and practical, random assignment helps separate an intervention from preexisting differences. Otherwise use credible observational designs, account for major confounders, and communicate uncertainty. The NIST/SEMATECH engineering statistics handbook provides accessible foundations for experimental design [R7].

Predefine the population, outcome, measurement window, and meaningful effect. Report uncertainty and practical importance, not just whether a p-value crosses a threshold. Watch for multiple comparisons when slicing results by dozens of segments.

## Leading indicators are hypotheses

A leading indicator is useful when it reliably anticipates a later outcome. It is not validated simply because managers find it intuitively appealing. Record the hypothesized causal relationship, evidence supporting it, and a plan to check whether it holds.

When renewal data is not yet available, early cancellation or complaint rates may provide provisional information. They are not interchangeable with actual renewal rates, and their predictive quality must be checked.

## Use evidence proportionate to stakes

Not every workflow tweak requires a randomized trial. Small reversible experiments can rely on lightweight observation. Large expensive or irreversible policy changes deserve stronger evidence. The standard should rise with cost, risk, and the opportunity for misleading inference.

**Practice exercise:** For a proposed activity-based KR, draw the chain from activity to customer or business outcome. Mark each untested link. Rewrite the objective as a discovery OKR if the most important link remains uncertain.
