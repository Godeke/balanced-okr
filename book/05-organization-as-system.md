# Chapter 5 — The Organization as a System

The most damaging OKR failures can arise when every department fulfills its own objective. Sales increases acquisition while underwriting absorbs exceptions; product teams ship features while operations inherits fragility. The aggregate outcome is worse even though each local dashboard is green.

## Ownership is not exclusive control

An outcome should have one accountable owner, but its delivery may require multiple contributors. For example, quote-to-bind conversion may depend on sales practices, underwriting policy, software usability, and response times.

Distinguish **accountable owner** (the person who explains the outcome), **contributing team** (a group committing specific work or capability), and **dependency** (a necessary condition not fully controlled by the owner). A dotted-line label alone does not allocate capacity or authority.

## Shared capacity is a portfolio constraint

Suppose four departments independently propose software-dependent OKRs. Each proposal is reasonable in isolation, but together they require twice the engineering capacity available. Approving all four creates an impossible promise.

Portfolio review should aggregate dependencies, compare priority and opportunity cost, and explicitly defer lower-value work. Teams should agree on deliverables, decision rights, sequencing, and the effect of changing priorities. “Development will support this” is not a commitment.

## Balance across the whole value stream

Guardrails should protect the system, not merely the immediate team. A customer service initiative that reduces average handling time can increase repeat contacts. A sales initiative that improves conversion can increase rework in underwriting. Track the downstream effect where it occurs.

This does not mean assigning every team an identical set of metrics. It means reviewing local objectives against shared outcomes and constraints.

## Bottom-up proposals with strategic coherence

Frontline employees often detect opportunities leadership misses. Encourage proposals and experimentation, but avoid a rigid quota of employee-originated OKRs. A mandated percentage becomes another metric to game.

An OKR menu can help newcomers, provided it offers reusable *patterns*—objective, evidence, likely failure modes, guardrails—rather than preapproved easy targets.

## When dependencies fail

Do not use cross-team dependencies as a universal excuse. Document the commitment, changes to capacity, decisions taken, and reasonable mitigation. Distinguish a team that could not control a dependency from one that failed to coordinate it.

**Practice exercise:** Draw a dependency map for three company-level outcomes. Identify shared teams, conflicting demands, and where a locally successful metric might shift costs to another group.
