# Chapter 5 — The Organization as a System

Every department can fulfill its own objective while customers or colleagues absorb the cost. Sales increases acquisition and underwriting inherits the exceptions. Product teams release features and operations inherits fragile systems. Look downstream as well as at the team setting the goal.

## The owner and the participants

An outcome should have one accountable owner, but its delivery may require multiple contributors. For example, quote-to-bind conversion may depend on sales practices, underwriting policy, software usability, and response times.

The **accountable owner** answers for the outcome and the decisions it requires. A **participant** is a person or team that agrees to contribute specific work or capacity. A **dependency** is something the outcome relies on, such as a carrier supplying renewal records. Putting a team's name on an OKR does little until someone with authority agrees to the work and its timing.

## Shared capacity is a portfolio constraint

Consider the [outreach pilot](04-causality-and-evidence.md). Sales needs to know which accounts were assigned an extra call, how much staff time they used, and which quoted accounts bound. The sales manager owns the pilot decision. Engineering can join the assignment records to binding data, but the same developer is due to start an underwriting dashboard.

At the portfolio review, the engineering lead offers one developer for two weeks to produce and check the pilot extract. The commercial and underwriting leads agree to move the dashboard start back two weeks. The engineering lead accepts the work, and the sales manager records the delivery date and informs the dashboard users. Engineering can now honor a commitment to another team's OKR while retaining ownership of its own delivery objectives. Had the dashboard taken priority, sales would have had to rescope or defer the pilot.

## Balance across the whole value stream

Choose a Guardrail where the harm would be felt. A customer service initiative that reduces average handling time can increase repeat contacts. A sales initiative that improves conversion can increase rework in underwriting. Ask the downstream team which deterioration would warrant action and who could act on it.

## Bottom-up proposals with strategic coherence

Frontline employees often detect opportunities leadership misses. In the pilot, a representative changed a proposed activity quota into a test the company was willing to resource. At the next portfolio review, ask which staff proposals changed a decision and which were deferred. A fixed percentage for employee-originated OKRs can invite teams to relabel who submitted them.

A menu of examples can help newcomers. Include the original Objective, the KR and Guardrail the team chose, and what the team learned. Ask the next team which assumptions still hold for its customers before it borrows the numbers.

## When dependencies fail

When a commitment slips, find the original agreement. What capacity changed? When did the owner hear about it? What could either team still do? Those details let a manager distinguish an external constraint from a coordination problem the teams can fix.

**Practice exercise.** Draw a dependency map for three company-level outcomes. Identify shared participants, conflicting demands, and where a locally successful measure could shift costs to another group.
