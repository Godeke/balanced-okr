# Balanced OKRs

Sales hit its acquisition target. Should we expand the campaign before we know whether those customers renew?

In *Measure What Matters*, John Doerr illustrates Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) with a pit crew that tracks both stop times and errors. Balanced OKRs builds on that practice by asking a team to agree in advance on the harm that would change its decision, who can act, and when the evidence will be ready. [Doerr’s pit-stop example](https://www.whatmatters.com/faqs/quantity-quality-key-results-pairing-examples)

| Part | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | The change you want for the business or its customers. |
| **Key Result (KR)** | The measure that shows progress, who or what it covers, its baseline and target, and the deadline. |
| **Guardrail** | The harm to watch for, the boundary or event that calls for action, who will review it, and when. |

## Should we expand the campaign?

The figures in both examples are hypothetical.

**Objective.** Grow new insurance business that customers choose to renew.

**Key Result.** Increase qualified new annual policies issued from 1,000 in the prior quarter to 1,150 in Q1 2027, ending March 31.

**Guardrail.** Before launch, choose the comparable prior-year Q1 cohort as the reference. Review expansion if the new cohort’s first-renewal rate falls more than two percentage points below that reference. Count all qualified new annual policies originally issued in each cohort. Early cancellations stay in the denominator.

| When | What the commercial director decides |
| --- | --- |
| March 31, 2027 | Sales issued 1,150 policies. Record the **KR as achieved** and the **Guardrail as pending**. The overall judgment is provisional. The director keeps the campaign at its current scope. Only the commercial director may authorize early expansion, recording the reason and a date to revisit it. The director owns the first-renewal review, due April 30, 2028. |
| At the first-renewal review | Of the Q1 2027 cohort, 82% renewed, compared with 86% for the prior-year Q1 cohort. The four-point decline breaches the Guardrail. The director pauses expansion and examines product mix, pricing, and whether the sales approach matched customers to suitable policies. The decline warrants a decision while its cause is still open. |

If the Objective were to improve retention, moving first renewal from 84% to 88% could itself be a KR. In the growth example, renewal sets a limit on what the business will accept. A breakdown by sales channel can help the director evaluate that Guardrail. Use such diagnostic analysis when the decision calls for it. A higher sales total cannot offset a material breach.

## How would this work for software?

One team wants approved changes to reach its quoting service sooner. Its **KR** is to cut median time from approval to production from 10 days last quarter to 5 days this quarter. Its **Guardrail** triggers a reliability review if more than 5% of the service’s production changes during the quarter require a rollback or hotfix in response to an incident. At review, the team can tell how quickly it shipped and what happened to reliability. Customer value would need evidence from customers’ use of the changes.

## Draft your own

Use one copy per Objective and return to it when the results arrive. [Copy the Balanced OKR worksheet](../book/worksheet.md)

Before adopting a measure, ask a frontline colleague how someone could hit it while customers lose. If a colleague questions a universal agent-call target, test whether calls improve conversion enough to justify staff time on small and complex accounts separately. If the test needs engineering, ask what capacity the team can commit and which other work will wait.

Keep OKR scores out of bonus formulas. An existing commission or public sales ranking can still reward volume. How a leader responds when a top seller reports a renewal problem tells the team whether raising concerns is welcome. At the next review, retire a measure if people can routinely improve it without improving the outcome.
